2. The one-minute story

A model telling

"The customer only waits for the payment result. Once the order transaction commits, Laravel dispatches a job. With the RabbitMQ driver that becomes a message published to an exchange, which routes it by bindings to queues such as fulfilment, email and stock sync. Workers, supervised and restarted on deploy, consume from those queues. For safety I want durable queues and persistent messages, manual acknowledgements so crashed workers' jobs are redelivered, and idempotent jobs because redelivery and retries mean a job can run twice. Jobs that run out of attempts land in failed_jobs, and RabbitMQ can dead-letter messages too."

3. Questions to ask them

Good questions show you know where the real decisions are. Pick three or four; do not recite the list.

AreaAsk
SetupWhich RabbitMQ driver and version do you use? Do you publish to your own exchange, and who declares queues and bindings?
ConsumingDo workers run queue:work or rabbitmq:consume? What prefetch is set? Do you use Horizon?
ReliabilityAre queues durable and messages persistent? Do you use publisher confirms? Is after_commit on?
FailureWhat are your tries and backoff? Who is alerted when jobs land in failed_jobs? Do you use dead letter exchanges or reroute_failed?
SafetyHow do you make fulfilment and email jobs safe to run twice?
OperationsWhat keeps workers running, and how does a deploy restart them? How do you watch queue depth?
AI toolsWhich parts of the queue code do AI tools help write, and how do you review that code?

4. Reviewing AI-written queue code

They are experimenting with AI tools, so you may be asked how you would check generated code. A model can write a job that runs perfectly in a demo and still fails under retries or restarts. Use this checklist, built from the lessons.

5. If they use something different

You were told RabbitMQ and Laravel, but not the driver, topology or tooling. If an answer differs from what you learned, do not bluff. Map it to the concepts: where does the message go, who acknowledges it, what happens on failure, what happens twice. Then say what you would check. "I have not seen that driver option; I would look at how it declares queues and acknowledges messages" is a strong answer because it shows method.

6. What I could not verify

  • The driver README does not state what happens when no exchange options are set, nor its defaults for durability, persistence, confirms, acknowledgement mode or prefetch.
  • retry_after is documented for Laravel's built-in drivers; I could not confirm it applies with this driver.
  • I do not know which driver, version or tooling the company runs. Treat the lessons as a framework for asking.
